# BeCore Tickets voice and visual direction

The recorded direction is **a knowing friend with standards**. On 10 August 2026 the user extended that voice across the whole product: discovery, checkout, tickets, confirmations, recovery, useful errors, submissions and instructions. It should sound like someone who knows a good Accra night and expects you to come along.

## Write like a person going out

Use recognisable situations: the indecisive group chat, picking an outfit, arriving at the door, finding your friends and the song everyone wants played. Keep the local music identity visible: Alté, Amapiano, day parties and late nights. Use natural contractions. Leave existing good lines alone.

The original event quips belong with the artwork: “Small room. Big decisions.”, “Sunset first. Regret nothing.”, “Dress like your ex might be there.” and “The shoes will not survive.”

## Make the action unmistakable

Personality applies everywhere; a joke never replaces the information someone needs. Say what happened, what the person needs to do and any consequence. Keep button labels, prices, fees, refund eligibility, payment references, dates, consent and access rules precise. Preserve test-commerce disclosures. Use sensitive reports and safety instructions to help the person, never to joke at their expense.

“Once is quite enough. Don’t pay again while we check your original payment” keeps both the voice and the required instruction. A failed request must show a failure, retain entered information and offer a usable next step. Native sharing opening is not proof that a message was delivered.

Avoid corporate phrasing such as “operational detail”, “first-party signals” and “moderation boundary” in customer copy. Don’t force a punchline into every label or repeat the same short-sentence rhythm across every section.
